# Jio Platforms Equity Valuation

An independent Excel valuation project combining a five-year operating forecast, discounted cash flow valuation and a Bharti Airtel peer comparison. The research cutoff is **31 October 2026**.

**[Download the Excel model](Jio_Platforms_DCF.xlsx?raw=true)**

## Model approach

Revenue is driven by subscriber growth and average revenue per user, with a residual component reconciling operating KPIs to consolidated revenue. The forecast links revenue to operating margins, cash taxes, depreciation, capital expenditure, lease investment and working capital to calculate free cash flow to the firm.

The valuation includes:

- Bear, base and bull operating and valuation assumptions.
- CAPM-based cost of equity, relevered beta and target capital weights.
- Mid-year discounting and a terminal value split equally between perpetual growth and an exit EV/EBITDA multiple.
- An enterprise-to-equity bridge covering borrowings, spectrum obligations, leases, excess cash, investments, minority interests and share dilution.
- WACC and terminal-growth sensitivity analysis.
- A separate Airtel operating and valuation comparison, plus reported broker-estimate cross-checks.

## Reviewing the workbook

Start with **Summary** for the selected scenario and investment considerations. **Forecast** and **Valuation** show the operating and valuation calculations. **Actuals** contains financial inputs and source references; **Method** explains assumptions and limitations. **Checks** contains reconciliations.

The workbook is saved with the **Bull** scenario selected. Set **Assumptions D5** to **1 = Bear, 2 = Base or 3 = Bull**. Open the workbook in Excel and recalculate with automatic calculation enabled, including What-If data tables. Native desktop Excel sensitivity-table behaviour has not been independently verified.

## Sources and limitations

Sources cited in the workbook include company financial disclosures, the RHP, investor presentations, market data and press summaries of broker research. Forecast assumptions are distinguished from reported financial inputs. Full broker models were unavailable; published targets are comparison points, not direct inputs to the DCF.

This is an operating forecast and valuation model, not an integrated three-statement model. Some balance-sheet components use earlier-period or book-value proxies, IPO costs are estimated, and peer reporting periods and business scopes differ. Cash-tax assumptions and the transition to terminal reinvestment require particular attention. These limitations remain documented in the workbook.
